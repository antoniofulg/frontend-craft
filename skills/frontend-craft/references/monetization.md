# Pricing, trials and expansion

Read for pricing pages, plan selection, trials, checkout, upgrade prompts and
account expansion. Apply [commercial strategy](commercial-strategy.md), including
its source terms. Review the approved offer and actual entitlements before
changing presentation; distinguish interface changes from proposed price,
billing or access-policy changes.

## Make the value comparison understandable

Identify the buying decision and each plan's audience, useful outcome, relevant
limit and total cost. Show consistent units, billing periods and important
differences. A monthly equivalent for annual billing must also explain the
annual commitment and amount charged. Retain material limits and terms beside
the decision rather than hiding them in unrelated help.

Consider an entry, recommended and advanced option when those roles represent
real customer needs. A target plan can gain prominence through comparison with
a relevant lower or higher tier, but neither exactly three plans nor a decoy
is mandatory. Each offered plan needs an honest description and a useful role;
do not invent a deliberately unusable tier or an unsupported popularity badge.

Lead comparisons with the attributes that explain fit and value; keep essential
differences accessible even when they weaken the preferred plan's sales pitch.
Explain the recommended option for this audience, and permit a different choice.
Test labels or ordering against purchase incidence and plan fit separately:
more selections of a high-priced tier may coincide with fewer purchases overall.

## Trials and checkout

Compare no-card trials, card-required trials, freemium or usage-limited access
against time needed to experience value, service cost, acquisition intent and
purchase requirements. Set trial boundaries from the product's usage cadence
and useful result rather than copying a competitor's day count.

Evaluate the entire funnel using comparable visitors or eligible accounts:
entry → trial → useful result → payment → retained use. Card collection may
increase trial-to-paid conversion while reducing trial starts enough to lower
overall revenue. Keep qualification effects and billing failures visible.

Explain what is available now, what triggers charging, the amount and cadence,
and how to cancel or change plans. Preserve approved terms and payment behavior.
For checkout or lead forms, inspect required fields, total cost, available
payment methods, validation, recovery and confirmation. Remove unnecessary
effort while retaining information needed to deliver or qualify the offer.
Use [interaction and accessibility](interaction-accessibility.md) for controls;
this reference does not supply payment-integration instructions.

## Offer expansion when the need is present

Locate a relevant moment: repeated useful use, an approaching capacity limit,
team collaboration or a requested capability. Connect the upgrade to the result
the user is trying to obtain, with the added entitlement and incremental cost.
Keep the interruption proportional to the action; avoid repeated unrelated
upsells during routine work.

Use actual usage and accumulated work to make value concrete. Loss framing is
appropriate only for a real, disclosed consequence of the current policy; a
free user's existing assets do not suddenly become forfeitable sales pressure.
Disclose paid boundaries before users invest effort in a workflow whose essential
result requires payment. Preserve work and provide an understandable return path
if the offer is declined.

For account expansion, distinguish seat, capacity and capability needs. More
invites or seats alone are not proof of value; examine active use and whether
the expansion supports the team's work. Necessary collaboration should reflect
the domain rather than manufacture dependency between departments.

## Validate net commercial impact

Use [experimentation](experimentation.md) to define eligible exposure and compare
paid conversion, revenue per eligible visitor/account and retained use. Include
refunds, billing complaints, downgrades and service costs where relevant. State
the observation window and whether later retention is still unknown.

If evidence is insufficient for a price experiment, propose customer research
or a clearly qualified pilot. Price or billing changes require that implementation
scope; a pricing-page review delivers recommendations, not new commercial terms.
