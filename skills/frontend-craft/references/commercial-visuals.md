# Visual composition for commercial decisions

Read when creating or reviewing the visual hierarchy of a landing page, pricing
offer, onboarding screen or activation feedback. Apply the audience, objective
and source terms in [commercial strategy](commercial-strategy.md). This guidance
adapts Richard's (@richardrx) ideas curated in Revenue-Centric Design; see
[sources](../SOURCES.md#commercial-expansion-in-050) and the
[preserved license](../licenses/revenue-centric-design.txt).

## Assign attention before choosing decoration

Identify what the visitor should understand first, what evidence supports that
understanding, and which action follows. Inspect the actual composition before
rewriting copy: competing emphasis, distant proof or an inconspicuous action can
weaken an otherwise clear message. Diagnose both argument and presentation rather
than assuming either is always the cause.

Use scale, weight, whitespace, grouping, position and relative contrast to give
the intended decision priority. A saturated button can still disappear among
equally saturated cards. Reduce competing emphasis before increasing every
element's size. Keep secondary actions visible at a lower emphasis appropriate
to their role; essential navigation and disclosures remain usable.

Use the project's tokens and approved visual direction to express this hierarchy.
Impeccable can supply art direction; this skill still makes composition decisions
without it. Explain any needed exception through the commercial objective and
authorized scope, rather than generating an unrelated palette or font system.

## Compose the promise, proof and action

Make the opening view communicate the offer, intended audience and next step.
Group the promise with a relevant demonstration and place the CTA where the
reading sequence makes its purpose clear. Avoid forcing everything into one
viewport at the expense of legibility. Common scanning patterns are hypotheses
about attention, not fixed coordinates for every language, device or audience.

Use section rhythm, headings and spacing to make objections and answers easy
to find. Put an applicable result, testimonial or reassurance beside the decision
it supports. Repeat the action after meaningful evidence on a long page, rather
than filling every section with competing buttons. Route claim verification and
public-page contracts to [landing pages](landing-pages.md).

Choose imagery for what it proves: a readable product view for a workflow, an
annotated detail for an unfamiliar mechanism, or an attributable customer example
for a result. Crop to the relevant detail without misrepresenting the product.
Use real people when their identity or experience adds relevant trust; a face is
not a substitute for proof, and a stock image cannot impersonate a customer.
Label mockups and illustrative data where viewers could mistake them for evidence.

## Express positioning visually

Translate the intended audience and buying context into tone, typography, form,
color and spacing using the approved direction. Compare category conventions:
retain cues buyers need to recognize the offer and differentiate where it helps
them understand the product's particular value. Distinctiveness earns its place
through recognition, trust or comprehension; novelty alone is not a commercial
result. A dense, direct sales page can fit an audience, as can a restrained one.
Judge either against the buyer's decision and observed outcomes.

Choose decoration and human imagery for relevance. A real customer's contextual
photo can reinforce their testimony, but a face alone proves neither trust nor
higher conversion. Treat upstream visual examples as compositions to investigate,
not templates or causal evidence. In particular, the cited Highrise experiment
compared page layouts with customer photos; it does not establish that employees
or real users universally outperform stock models.

For checkout summary, reassurance and form effort, read [checkout](checkout.md).
For operational KPI hierarchy and actions, read [dashboards](dashboards.md);
marketing prominence is not a substitute for accurate data representation.

## Compare compositions when direction is open

For a new public page or an authorized change of direction, use the
[subject inventory](landing-pages.md#ground-the-visual-concept-in-the-subject)
to compare a small set of genuinely different compositions. Two or three can
clarify a real choice; a clear approved direction needs no alternatives exercise.
Use relevant, source-linked page examples or an available curated reference to
show geometry and hierarchy rather than relying on adjectives such as premium.
Inspect the relevant example or image; a title or remembered trend is not evidence
of its layout. If browsing is unavailable, describe proposals as proposals.

Compare each option by its argument, dominant element, actual copy length, proof
placement, available imagery, mobile reading order and implementation effort.
Reject a composition whose assumptions require invented testimonials, extra plans
or missing product photography. State why the selected option fits the subject
and what compatible owners/tokens it reuses. A familiar layout is acceptable when
it best serves those constraints; randomness is not evidence of fit.

Use Impeccable's existing design workflow and context for this decision when
available. Save an authorized choice in the existing brief instead of creating a
second design system. A companion's library or generator can supply candidates,
but its palettes, aesthetic bans and workflows do not override approved direction,
accessibility or task scope. Consultation does not authorize copying a collection.
Reuse the project's visual vocabulary in ordinary refinements and operational UI.

## Make pricing visually comparable

Align corresponding plan attributes, units and billing periods so users can
compare without reconstructing the offer. Lead with the differentiators that
explain fit; place a relevant deciding benefit near the action. Retain material
limits and total charges in readable text.

Give a justified recommended plan prominence through a restrained combination
of position, border/background, scale or a truthful label. Preserve contrast,
readability and equivalent access to other plans. Use the offer's real structure
to choose the number of cards; styling does not justify inventing a tier.

A distinctive price treatment can be an experiment for a genuine promotion,
including the upstream suggestion of less-fluent typography. Treat it as a
testable option, not a default: maintain readable numbers, currency, period and
terms, and compare comprehension as well as purchases. Apply
[monetization](monetization.md) for offer and complete-funnel decisions.

## Show the path and acknowledge useful progress

Compose empty states around the useful result and one clear next action. An
example preview can show the destination; distinguish it visibly from real
account activity. Keep the current step, completed work and remaining necessary
effort understandable without making the screen a tour of every feature.

At a meaningful first result, make success perceptible and show the next useful
action. A changed result view, concise summary or proportional celebration can
reinforce achievement. Preserve accurate data; an animated rising chart needs
actual values that support the shape. Follow [activation](activation.md) for the
result definition and [motion](motion.md) for interruption and reduced motion.

## Inspect composition and validate the hypothesis

Check desktop and mobile reading order, realistic text, image crops, CTA
visibility and nearby proof. Stacked pricing cards must retain comparable labels,
charges and recommendation context. Sticky elements must not conceal terms,
content or focus. Use [interaction and accessibility](interaction-accessibility.md)
for contrast, keyboard and target checks.

For each visual recommendation, identify the element, the attention or comprehension
problem, the concrete composition change and the expected commercial mechanism.
Use the existing [commercial findings](review.md#commercial-findings) format.
Mark screenshot findings as visual observations; task behavior requires interaction
evidence, and sales impact requires [commercial validation](experimentation.md).
Use the [rendered critique](review.md#critique-the-rendered-composition) for visual
inspection and corrections as part of the existing review pass.
