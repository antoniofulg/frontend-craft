# Commercial strategy and behavioral choices

Read for commercial interface creation or review: conversion, activation,
monetization, expansion and retention. This reference owns the commercial brief
and decision rules; journey references apply them to a particular surface.

This commercial guidance adapts ideas from Richard ([@richardrx](https://x.com/richardrx)),
as curated in [Revenue-Centric Design](https://github.com/heliocosta-dev/revenue-centric-design).
The [upstream terms](../licenses/revenue-centric-design.txt) apply to this file
and the commercial guidance derived from it. Do not apply that material to
betting, casino, gambling or other excluded real-money games-of-chance projects,
including the loot-box mechanics named in those terms. Decline that application
and explain the source restriction. Attribution and adaptation details are in
[sources](../SOURCES.md#commercial-guidance-in-040).

## Establish the outcome

Recover these facts from the request, product context and available evidence.
Keep them in the existing brief or response; a separate strategy document is
unnecessary for a page review.

- **Outcome:** purchase, qualified opportunity, activation, retained revenue or
  expansion. Name the user action that contributes to it and the relevant cohort.
- **Audience:** user, buyer and approver where different; purchase trigger,
  current alternative, desired result, constraints and material objections.
- **Decision stage:** discovering a problem, comparing approaches, evaluating
  this product or ready to transact. Match the argument and next action to it.
- **Offer and proof:** actual capabilities, price/terms, credible demonstration
  and supplied evidence. The [landing-page claim inventory](landing-pages.md#commercial-brief-and-claim-inventory)
  owns claim verification; reuse it when writing commercial copy.
- **Baseline:** what is known about entry, abandonment, payment and subsequent
  use; distinguish observed behavior from assumptions. Ask only for facts whose
  absence blocks the task. Missing analytics still permits a useful review.

For example, a demo page may need more qualified opportunities per visitor,
while a self-serve product may need more visitors reaching a paid, useful result.
A higher form completion rate alone does not establish either outcome.

## Diagnose before selecting a tactic

Trace entry context → promise → decision → action → delivered value. Inspect
the requested path far enough to understand what the CTA actually produces.
Identify where effort, uncertainty, missing proof or an offer mismatch could
prevent the desired action. Use drop-off data to locate a problem; use observed
behavior, interviews or a suitable experiment to investigate its cause.

Choose the relevant branch: [landing pages](landing-pages.md),
[activation](activation.md), [monetization](monetization.md) or
[retention](retention.md). Load [experimentation](experimentation.md) when choosing
metrics or a validation method. Interface observations alone establish neither
commercial uplift nor the cause of churn.

## Resolve commercial and implementation choices

The commercial objective guides what to change and why. Existing components,
page families and Impeccable's direction guide how to express the change.
When an established pattern obstructs the objective, propose a bounded exception
with its evidence, expected mechanism and validation method. A familiar layout
is not sufficient reason to retain an ineffective decision path.

Implement that exception when the requested scope authorizes the change; reviews
return a proposal. Preserve accessibility, truthful information, functional
contracts and explicit project requirements. Where a proposal changes an
approved visual direction or commercial term outside scope, describe the needed
decision instead of silently changing it. Ordinary repairs still reuse the
existing family without an unrelated conversion workshop.

## Apply behavioral mechanisms with context

Use these as candidate explanations and experiments, not guaranteed effects.
Name the mechanism only when it clarifies why a concrete change could help.

| Mechanism | Candidate application | Boundary and evidence to watch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Defaults | Preselect a configuration appropriate to the user's stated task | Make the choice and financial consequences visible and editable; watch corrections, refunds and satisfaction as well as acceptance |
| Anchoring and relative comparison | Compare plans against a relevant alternative using consistent units | Use real prices and meaningful differences; check paid revenue and plan fit, not only selection of the promoted tier |
| Choice reduction | Give the next useful action visual priority and group secondary options | Preserve necessary alternatives and navigation; choose option count from real needs rather than a universal three-plan rule |
| Progress and commitment | Show completed work and a reachable next useful step | Credit actual progress; avoid fabricated completion percentages or extra tasks that merely create investment |
| Gain/loss framing | Explain a concrete benefit or an actual consequence of changing access | State accurate scope and timing; distinguish loss of access from deletion, and retain an understandable choice |
| Salience and proof | Place a relevant demonstration or verifiable result beside a material objection | Match proof to this audience and claim; visual prominence cannot substitute for evidence |

Urgency requires an actual deadline or constraint. A persuasive design must still
let users understand price, consent, alternatives and cancellation. Avoid adding
dependency, inaccessible text or obstructive steps merely to increase switching
cost. Evaluate downstream value and trust alongside immediate conversion.

## Deliver the decision

For a plan, state the outcome, barrier, proposed change, supporting evidence and
validation. For a review, use the commercial finding format in
[review](review.md#commercial-findings); do not produce a second report. For an
implementation, report the shipped change and functional checks separately from
commercial measurement that remains pending.
