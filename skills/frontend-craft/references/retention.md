# Retention, renewal and cancellation

Read for churn diagnosis, ongoing value communication, renewal and cancellation
flows. Apply [commercial strategy](commercial-strategy.md), including its source
terms; use [activation](activation.md) when users never reached first value.

## Diagnose the departure

Separate users who never activated from users who received value and later left.
Inspect available cohort behavior, reasons, support evidence and the promise
that brought them in. Early cancellation is a clue, not proof of one cause.

| Evidence to investigate | Candidate response |
| --- | --- |
| Users stall before their first useful result | Repair the activation barrier |
| Advertised result or effort differs from delivery | Correct the promise or the actual delivery gap |
| Users obtained a result but cannot see recurring value | Explain relevant ongoing value, or reassess whether the job is recurring |
| Errors, inaccessible controls or repeated support questions impede use | Repair the owning interaction and recovery path |
| Budget, seasonality or team needs changed | Assess a fitting downgrade, pause or different offer |
| Failed payments or entitlement mistakes caused departure | Diagnose billing/recovery separately from voluntary churn |

A completed one-time job can justify leaving. Repeated notifications or artificial
tasks do not create a recurring need. Retention proposals should explain the
continuing user benefit, not merely increase the inconvenience of leaving.

## Make continuing value visible

Use actual results, relevant history, useful personalization and working
integrations to explain what the product contributes. Distinguish real customer
outcomes from activity counts and estimated savings. Announce relevant new
capabilities or migration options where they address a known need, following
the user's communication preferences and the task's authorization.

Recommend reminders when they help complete a chosen task; use honest timing
and consequences. Preserve data access/export contracts. A proposed reminder
does not authorize sending messages or installing new tracking.

## Support a clear cancellation decision

Show what changes, when it changes, final charges where applicable, and the
actual policy for stored data and access. Distinguish subscription cancellation,
account deletion and loss of a paid capability. Preserve approved terms.

Offer a relevant pause, downgrade or recovery option when it fits the stated
reason. Keep the cancellation path discoverable and operable without accepting
the alternative. A reason question may help diagnosis, but should not become
an unrelated obstacle to completing the requested exit.

Confirm the outcome and effective date. Inspect keyboard navigation, pending
state, retries and confirmation with [interaction and accessibility](interaction-accessibility.md).
Loss framing must describe a real consequence; do not invent deletion, urgency
or charges to discourage departure.

## Measure retention after the intervention

Use comparable cohorts and a defined observation window. Track continued useful
use and retained revenue alongside completed cancellations, support complaints
and refunds. Distinguish a temporary pause or delayed cancellation from a retained
customer. Define whether churn counts accounts, users or revenue; expansion can
mask customer losses in a net revenue metric. Read
[experimentation](experimentation.md) for validation and causal limits.
