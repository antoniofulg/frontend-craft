# Retention, renewal and cancellation

Read for churn diagnosis, ongoing value communication, renewal and cancellation
flows. Apply [commercial strategy](commercial-strategy.md), including its source
terms; use [activation](activation.md) when users never reached first value.

## Diagnose the departure

Separate users who never activated from users who received value and later left.
Inspect available cohort behavior, reasons, support evidence and the promise
that brought them in. Early cancellation is a clue, not proof of one cause.

| Evidence to investigate | Candidate response |
| --- | --- |
| Users stall before their first useful result | Repair the activation barrier |
| Advertised result or effort differs from delivery | Correct the promise or the actual delivery gap |
| Users obtained a result but cannot see recurring value | Explain relevant ongoing value, or reassess whether the job is recurring |
| Errors, inaccessible controls or repeated support questions impede use | Repair the owning interaction and recovery path |
| Budget, seasonality or team needs changed | Assess a fitting downgrade, pause or different offer |
| Failed payments or entitlement mistakes caused departure | Diagnose billing/recovery separately from voluntary churn |

A completed one-time job can justify leaving. Repeated notifications or artificial
tasks do not create a recurring need. Retention proposals should explain the
continuing user benefit, not merely increase the inconvenience of leaving.

Inspect the adjacent funnel before assigning a cause: weak visit-to-signup can
reflect audience/source or promise; weak trial-to-paid can reflect activation,
paywall timing or plan value. Match the intervention to the observed stage and
use [monetization](monetization.md) for commercial boundaries. Compare advertised
results, setup effort and team size with actual delivery; positive feedback from
survivors can coexist with expectation-driven early departures.

## Connect retention to acquisition payback

Compare cohort survival with the time needed to recover acquisition cost from
contribution after service costs. Count customers who leave before payback; an
activation improvement can help both retention and acquisition economics, while
price, channel quality and service cost remain separate possible causes. Use
[pricing economics](pricing-economics.md) when the offer's economics need modeling.

To size a retention hypothesis with stable inputs: monthly lost accounts =
eligible base × monthly account churn; avoided losses = lost accounts × assumed
relative reduction; preserved MRR = avoided losses × monthly revenue per retained
account. Label the reduction as an assumption until measured. MRR × 12 is an
annualized run-rate, not twelve months of guaranteed cash or profit. Model future
survival, margin and expansion separately for cohort value; use actual account
revenue when a single average would distort the estimate.

## Make continuing value visible

Use actual results, relevant history, useful personalization and working
integrations to explain what the product contributes. Distinguish real customer
outcomes from activity counts and estimated savings. Announce relevant new
capabilities or migration options where they address a known need, following
the user's communication preferences and the task's authorization.

Recommend reminders when they help complete a chosen task; use honest timing
and consequences. Preserve data access/export contracts. A proposed reminder
does not authorize sending messages or installing new tracking.

## Build useful habits and organizational continuity

Identify an existing recurring job or life event that justifies return. A seasonal
product may fit an annual occasion; a completed one-time task may require a
different offer rather than more notifications. Match reminders and goals to the
job's cadence, with an action that produces a visible useful result. Test sustained
outcomes and notification fatigue, not login frequency alone. Relevant new insights
can motivate return; manufacture neither scarcity nor unpredictable rewards merely
to keep attention.

Preserve familiar shortcuts and mental models where they support efficient work.
Let personalization, searchable history and working integrations increase useful
value over time. Explain that value while retaining export and access contracts;
retention obtained through obstructed exit is different from voluntary preference.

For team products, identify the risk of one champion owning all knowledge, access
or setup. Support shared ownership, appropriate roles, handover and collaboration
where the real workflow benefits. Measure adoption across relevant roles and
whether work continues when a champion leaves. Shared approvals or cross-team
reports should serve an actual dependency rather than inventing one to prevent exit.
A useful customer community can support learning and belonging when the audience
has that need; evaluate participation and outcomes before investing in it.

## Repair communication and support causes

Check whether target users understand labels, setup instructions and the promised
outcome. Replace internal jargon with their vocabulary and test comprehension with
less experienced users; assumed familiarity can masquerade as a feature gap.

Cluster support contacts by confusion, defect, missing capability and buyer-fit
problem. Add contextual answers or fix the owning interaction for repeated issues;
keep escalation for real exceptions. Lower ticket volume is useful only alongside
successful resolution, satisfaction and continued use. Cheap or impulse offers may
change support needs; verify this with the product's audience and costs.

When shipping a relevant capability or replacement product, identify affected
active customers and offer a clear discovery or migration path before they ask
to cancel. Explain changed value, effort and terms. Measure qualified awareness,
adoption and successful migration; release-email delivery alone is insufficient.
Proposing outreach does not authorize sending it.

## Support a clear cancellation decision

Show what changes, when it changes, final charges where applicable, and the
actual policy for stored data and access. Distinguish subscription cancellation,
account deletion and loss of a paid capability. Preserve approved terms.

Offer a relevant pause, downgrade or recovery option when it fits the stated
reason. Keep the cancellation path discoverable and operable without accepting
the alternative. A reason question may help diagnosis, but should not become
an unrelated obstacle to completing the requested exit. Offer optional free-text
feedback when preset reasons miss the real issue. A temporary credit or extension
can be tested when economically justified; check whether it restores useful use
or only postpones the same cancellation.

Confirm the outcome and effective date. Inspect keyboard navigation, pending
state, retries and confirmation with [interaction and accessibility](interaction-accessibility.md).
Loss framing must describe a real consequence; do not invent deletion, urgency
or charges to discourage departure.

## Measure retention after the intervention

Use comparable cohorts and a defined observation window. Track continued useful
use and retained revenue alongside completed cancellations, support complaints
and refunds. Distinguish a temporary pause or delayed cancellation from a retained
customer. Define whether churn counts accounts, users or revenue; expansion can
mask customer losses in a net revenue metric. Read
[experimentation](experimentation.md) for validation and causal limits.
