# Images, footage and product demonstrations

Read when selecting, sourcing, generating, cropping or reviewing visual media.
For a commercial page, use its existing claim inventory and approved direction;
for a narrow asset repair, keep the scope to that asset and its consumers.
This reference owns media selection, provenance and delivery. Commercial
hierarchy remains in [commercial visuals](commercial-visuals.md).

## Inventory before composing

Locate the project's actual photographs, illustrations, screenshots, video and
media owners. Record what is usable now, what requires permission or replacement,
and which source files can support alternate crops. Separate product evidence
from contextual imagery, generated illustration and prototype content.

Choose each asset by its job: explain a workflow, reveal a material, establish a
place, identify a real person or support a specific claim. Inspect the image or
clip itself, including its proposed crop; search titles and metadata cannot prove
what is visible. A short page can legitimately use one image or typography alone.
The number and format follow the information needed, not a media quota.

Use the subject's own material when it communicates the point. If it is missing,
consider a verified product capture, relevant archival/open material, a clearly
illustrative generated image or a purpose-built diagram. Select by relevance,
accuracy and available rights rather than a universal ordering of these sources.
An honest static explanation is preferable to unrelated stock or a fake product
image. Do not invent claims or product details to fill the layout.

## Source relevant material

Search the subject's actual context: place and era, material, process, output or
trade terminology. Open collections such as Wikimedia Commons, Openverse and
museum archives can offer useful context. Consult the original item page and
current usage terms, not only an aggregator's result or a direct download URL.
Use project search/browser tools or an available asset helper; no particular
service, API key or bundled script is required.

Prefer a coherent family of images with enough detail and compatible light,
era and treatment. Inspect resolution at the intended display size and crop.
Do not imply that an archival scene or stock person depicts this client's own
premises, team, customers or products. Supply context when viewers might confuse
an illustration, reconstruction or historical view with current product evidence.

For generated material, use an available image tool when authorized by the task.
Specify the subject, composition, lighting, intended crop and relationship to the
other assets. Verify the result visually for false product features, distorted
objects, misleading text and inconsistent identity. Keep illustrative imagery
distinct from testimonials, real staff, product captures and measured results.

## Keep provenance with the asset

Use the existing asset manifest or credits mechanism. If none exists and assets
are added, retain a small record beside them or in project documentation:

| Field | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Local asset and role | File/variant, page placement and what it communicates |
| Origin | Original item/source page, creator and title when supplied |
| Usage basis | Applicable license/permission and its source; unknown means unresolved |
| Changes | Crop, recolor, composite or other relevant modifications |
| Credit and representation | Required wording/link and its location; illustrative or historical context where needed |

Read the applicable terms before use, including modification, attribution and
redistribution conditions. An open search result does not resolve every right;
name uncertainties and choose a usable alternative when they block inclusion.
Keep required notices in the delivered project, not only in the agent's report.
Use existing credits rather than adding decorative captions to every image.
For generated or project-owned assets, record their actual origin and usage basis
instead of inventing a public license or creator.

Store usable assets through the project's existing media pipeline. Keep source
and attribution links even when a file is downloaded. Prefer stable project
hosting or the established media service over arbitrary external hotlinks;
follow the source's permitted delivery mechanism. Verify downloaded files are
the expected media and inspect them before inclusion. A failed search or download
is a limitation to report, not permission to substitute misleading material.

## Show the actual software

Capture the real product state with authorized access and synthetic or sanitized
data, or compose a scoped demonstration using its real components and owners.
Choose a readable detail that proves the point. Preserve enough context to make
the action and result understandable; avoid shrinking a whole dashboard until its
text becomes decoration. Mark illustrative values and unconnected prototypes
where they could be mistaken for shipped behavior.

Keep product fragment styling within its own scope and retain its established
states and semantics. A marketing composition does not authorize restyling the
underlying application. For interactive examples, follow
[useful demonstrations](landing-pages.md#demonstrate-value-through-a-useful-interaction).
Never expose customer identifiers, credentials or private account details in a
capture; check the exported file as well as the rendered crop.

## Purpose-built diagrams and illustrations

When a diagram or illustration communicates something available media cannot,
depict a named object, process or result from the subject. Establish a coherent
visual vocabulary: shape, stroke/fill treatment, palette, perspective and level
of detail. Make the main structure readable first, then add the distinguishing
parts and smaller informative details. Inspect both the intended display size
and the mobile version; detail that becomes visual noise should be simplified.
Polish is not a reason to invent product features, mechanisms or physical details.

Choose the medium by the actual job and available tools: a structured diagram
can use native markup or SVG; richer illustrative artwork can use an image tool.
Use real labels and meaningful relationships rather than decorative interface
bars. Keep necessary labels readable, translatable and available as text, and
provide an equivalent explanation for information conveyed visually. Use the
existing illustration style when one exists. A product concept or reconstruction
is identified as illustrative, not presented as a photograph or shipped result.

## Prepare and inspect delivery

Use a consistent treatment that preserves the information the image must convey.
Choose crops per viewport when the subject or text area needs it. Preserve the
important object, face or UI result on a phone; check overlays against the actual
image rather than a convenient background sample. Keep text in HTML when it must
adapt, translate or remain selectable. Provide contextual alternative text for
informative media and the appropriate empty alternative for decorative images.

Use the project's existing image optimization and responsive delivery. Supply
appropriate dimensions and reserve layout space; match resolution and format to
the displayed role. Give important opening media suitable loading priority and
defer offscreen media where appropriate. Verify decoding/loading, missing-media
behavior, layout shifts and real crops in the supported environments. Use
[performance](performance.md) when media affects loading or responsiveness.

Add footage when movement explains a process, place or result better than a
still. Inspect the clip, its audio, looping seam, file size and poster frame.
Supply captions/transcripts or description when needed for equivalent access to
information. Respect reduced-motion preferences, provide appropriate playback
controls and keep the main content usable when autoplay is blocked or video fails.
Follow [motion](motion.md) for movement and [interaction and accessibility](interaction-accessibility.md)
for behavior. A static image is valid when footage adds no useful evidence.

Before delivery, check the actual assets, crops, required credits and fallback
states. List unresolved replacements or permissions and the checks not performed.
An asset's presence, license metadata or successful HTTP response alone does not
prove visual suitability, truthful representation or a working rendered result.
