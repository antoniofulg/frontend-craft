# Landing pages

Read for public marketing pages or reviews of a visitor's conversion journey.
Keep operational dashboard patterns separate from marketing composition.
For creation or conversion review, first read
[commercial strategy](commercial-strategy.md) for the objective, audience,
behavioral choices and source terms. A narrow public-page repair only needs its
affected contracts. Reviews also use [commercial findings](review.md#commercial-findings).
For page creation or visual/conversion review, read
[commercial visuals](commercial-visuals.md) for hierarchy, proof imagery, CTA
placement and responsive composition; a copy-only edit need not load it.
For crawling, indexing, initial public HTML, sitemap or AI discovery work, read
[technical SEO and discovery](technical-seo.md). It owns those implementation
checks; this reference owns the visitor's decision and public redesign inventory.

## Establish the visitor's decision

Identify audience, traffic context when known, offer, primary next action, and
the factual evidence available. Let Impeccable own visual direction. Reuse brand
tokens and applicable public-site components without forcing the dashboard shell
or its density onto a landing page.

Build a content sequence that answers the visitor's actual questions: what is
offered, why it matters, how it works, whether to trust it, what it costs where
relevant, and what happens next. The sequence follows the decision; it is not a
mandatory nine-section template or a fixed word-count formula.

## Ground the visual concept in the subject

For page creation or a redesign with an open visual direction, collect the
material that can make this subject recognizable: its actual output, distinctive
workflow, name, place, materials, constraints, rituals or customer vocabulary.
Use verified details from the brief, product and assets; a numerical inventory
quota is unnecessary. Distinguish facts from ideas still requiring confirmation.
Reuse the existing product/design brief and Impeccable context rather than
creating a parallel identity document.

Connect one of those details to an organizing visual idea and the visitor's
decision. For example, a dispatch product's actual handover record could organize
a page about traceable deliveries; generic moving parcels do not explain that
product's particular advantage. Explain the connection in a sentence and use it
to select composition and evidence, not merely a hero decoration.

Test the idea against a relevant known competitor: would swapping the name leave
the argument and imagery equally accurate? If so, look for a more specific
connection in the supplied evidence. State when no competitor evidence is
available; a hypothetical name swap tests specificity, not verified market
uniqueness. Keep familiar category cues that help buyers understand the offer.
Carry the useful idea through later sections without forcing a motif where it
hurts comprehension. Use [composition alternatives](commercial-visuals.md#compare-compositions-when-direction-is-open)
when the visual decision remains open and [imagery](imagery.md) when media must
be selected, sourced or prepared.

With an approved direction, refine within it. A review can identify a generic
execution and propose a change; a narrow repair does not reopen art direction.

## Match the argument to the decision

Use the audience's awareness and traffic context from the commercial brief.
For unfamiliar problems, demonstrate the situation and why it matters; for
solution comparisons, explain relevant tradeoffs; for product evaluation, supply
specific proof and fit; near purchase, clarify terms, risk and the next step.
Select the actual barrier rather than forcing every visitor through every stage.

Give the primary action clear visual priority and wording that matches its
destination. Place proof and objection answers near the decisions they support.
Repeat a CTA when a long page creates another meaningful decision point; choose
frequency from reading flow rather than a fixed section interval. Retain useful
comparison paths, policy links and necessary navigation.

Evaluate the full requested path through the mobile form, checkout or booking
confirmation where accessible. A button click is an intermediate metric when
the objective is a qualified opportunity or purchase. Route plan/trial decisions
to [monetization](monetization.md), purchase/form completion to
[checkout](checkout.md), and post-signup value to
[activation](activation.md) only when they fall within the review scope.

## Diagnose comprehension, qualification and trust

Show the opening view briefly to a representative unfamiliar buyer, then ask
what the product does, who it serves and why it matters. Record their actual
answer; an agent's own interpretation is not a user test. Choose pain-led,
outcome-led or category-led wording by awareness, then check comprehension.

Separate cold traffic from followers, referrals and returning visitors when
comparing conversion. Changed audience mix can change the rate without a page
regression; investigate page, channel and offer together. Recover customer
vocabulary and objections from sales conversations, interviews, support and
reviews before copying a successful competitor's composition. In early products,
a concrete buying objection and willingness to commit are stronger commercial
evidence than compliments; route market validation to the positioning reference
when that work is in scope.

Qualify through specific use cases, honest fit/limits, visible pricing where
appropriate, and a necessary qualification question. Compare qualified outcomes,
closed deals and sales effort, not raw leads alone. Fewer leads at the same spend
and same closed-deal count do not by themselves lower acquisition cost.

Put relevant reassurance beside the CTA: actual next step, expected effort,
cost and commitment. Match verbs to the journey, from exploring a demonstration
to confirming a purchase. Claims such as time-to-result, no-card trials, refunds,
free shipping and remaining inventory require supporting facts and readable
conditions. Remove redundant steps while keeping an informed purchase decision.

Prefer proof buyers can verify: named customers and roles, attributable reviews,
relevant results with scope, demonstrations and case studies. Preserve authentic
criticism and rating distributions; never manufacture a target star rating or
infer that a recognizable logo proves a particular outcome. Offer concise proof
near the opening promise and deeper evidence where buyers need it. Keep useful
verification links; reduce distracting exits by relevance rather than banning
all outbound links.

For feature pages, front-load scannable benefits and an accessible demonstration
when it helps explain the workflow. Keep detailed limits and technical evidence
reachable for evaluators. The number of bullets, sections and repeated actions
follows the decision, language and device rather than an arbitrary quota.

## Public redesign contracts

Before replacing an existing public page, inventory the affected contracts from
routes, rendered markup, metadata, form handlers and analytics callers. Record
what stays and any deliberate migration in the existing brief or change plan:

- URLs, query parameters and shared deep links; preserve destinations or map old
  URLs to intended replacements using the project's redirect mechanism.
- Anchor IDs and incoming fragment links; retain working targets or provide a
  compatible target at the replacement section. A page redirect alone does not
  repair a missing fragment target.
- Titles, descriptions, canonical URLs, robots directives, social previews,
  structured data and applicable locale/sitemap entries. Check their rendered
  output and consistency with the replacement content.
- Analytics event names, payloads, consent conditions and firing points; preserve
  attribution and avoid firing a conversion twice after a composition change.
- Form field names, requiredness, hidden attribution values, validation, submit
  destinations and success/error behavior; visual similarity is not payload
  compatibility.
- Legal text, consent choices, policy links, pricing and disclaimers. Carry
  approved meaning forward; changes follow the authorized migration scope.

Verify representative old URLs/anchors, metadata, form submissions and relevant
events through existing safe preview/test mechanisms. Record unverified contracts
and intentional changes. A visual redesign alone does not authorize changing
commercial/legal terms or publishing a migration.

## Commercial brief and claim inventory

Before writing new copy, recover the audience's situation, desired outcome,
current alternative, decision stage and material objections from supplied context.
Distinguish user, buyer and approver when they need different evidence. Ask only
for missing facts that would otherwise force an invented offer or claim.

Maintain a small working inventory for claims used on the page. Return it in the
response for planning or review; update an existing brief only when the requested
work authorizes editing it. Use this shape rather than a second marketing ledger:

| Claim | Evidence and scope | Qualification | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Proposed promise, number, testimonial or guarantee | Supplied source, date/population and permission where relevant | Limits needed for an accurate reading | Supported, needs confirmation, or omit |

A design example is not evidence for a customer count. Distinguish an aspiration
from a product capability already available. Keep the promise, supporting proof,
CTA destination and post-click experience aligned; an honest narrower promise
is preferable to an unsupported stronger one. Flag contradictions with approved
copy rather than silently changing commercial facts.

## Narrative and alternatives

Outline sections as visitor question → answer → evidence → next decision. For
example, an integration concern may need a concrete workflow illustration before
a purchase CTA; a familiar low-friction product may need a much shorter path.
Remove sections that repeat a claim without adding useful proof or resolving an
objection. Preserve the public-site's established header, navigation and vocabulary.

When asked to explore messaging, offer a small set of headline/CTA alternatives
on one meaningful axis, such as outcome-led versus workflow-led framing. Each
option must use the same supported facts and state its audience fit or tradeoff.
CTA wording describes the actual next step: booking a conversation differs from
starting a trial. Avoid arbitrary word counts, fake urgency and changing the offer
to make an option sound stronger. Save chosen wording to the existing brief only
within authorized edits; otherwise present it for review in the response.

When only implementation or visual refinement is requested, preserve approved
copy. Do not automatically run a messaging workshop or produce alternatives.

## Make each element earn its place

- Make the principal promise and next action easy to locate. Use supporting
  text only when it adds necessary information rather than paraphrasing a title.
- Choose imagery that demonstrates the product or desired outcome. A clear
  product screenshot can be stronger evidence than decorative lifestyle imagery.
- Use real, attributable proof supplied or verified for the task. Do not invent
  testimonials, customer logos, metrics, scarcity, guarantees or certifications.
- Give one action clear priority where that matches the decision. Secondary
  paths may be useful for different readiness levels; distinguish their purpose.
- Explain what clicking does. Request only information needed at that stage,
  and make failure recovery and the post-submit next step understandable.
- Answer material objections near the decision they affect. Avoid repeating
  generic trust badges or adding urgency without a factual reason.

Check reading order, hierarchy, contrast, meaningful images, mobile composition
and keyboard flow. Preserve the core offer when adapting layouts; avoid overlays
or sticky actions that obstruct content or focus. Load the interaction reference
for forms/controls and the motion reference if movement is involved.

## Demonstrate value through a useful interaction

Consider an interactive demonstration when the buying question depends on an
input or an observable result: preview a real finish, explore an actual output,
or calculate a quote using documented rules. Name the question it answers and
compare the benefit with a clear static example. Choose the static example when
interaction adds effort, unsupported behavior or maintenance without useful proof.
An interaction is an option, not a requirement for every landing page.

Reuse the real product owner or supported calculation where available. Use
representative, explicitly illustrative data when appropriate; a prototype must
not imply a live capability, current availability, actual quotation or saved order.
Rates, eligibility and commercial terms come from verified inputs. Preserve the
real primary destination; a demonstration must not impersonate checkout or submit
customer data to an invented endpoint.

Keep the result understandable with keyboard and touch, visible labels and
feedback, and without relying on hover or motion. Supply an equivalent static
example, explanation or reachable destination when scripting/media is unavailable;
required disclosure and the main action remain accessible. Verify changed inputs,
empty/invalid values, reset, slow/error states where relevant and the fallback.
Use [interaction and accessibility](interaction-accessibility.md) and
[motion](motion.md) only for the behavior involved. Report an unconnected demo as
such; commercial benefit remains a hypothesis until evaluated.

## Review by evidence

When traffic/ad context exists, compare the promise before the click with the
page and the action after it. Review the whole requested path, including form
errors and confirmation, rather than ending at an attractive hero.

Separate observed usability defects from conversion hypotheses. Analytics can
locate drop-off but does not by itself establish the cause. State missing data
and distinguish a proposed experiment from a proven improvement. Do not predict
percentage lifts, infer real-user speed from a single local trace, or treat
insufficient traffic as a reason to skip a useful qualitative review.

Treat loading and interaction performance as possible commercial bottlenecks.
When slowness is observed or measured, use [performance](performance.md) to
identify technical causes and [experimentation](experimentation.md) to evaluate
the business outcome under comparable traffic. A lab grade or another company's
reported lift is not evidence of this page's revenue impact.

Prioritize concrete changes by likely user impact, supporting evidence and
effort. Name the element, failure, proposed correction and what would validate
it. Respect the offer and approved copy; improving layout does not authorize
changing commercial terms or installing analytics infrastructure.

For a conversion audit, prioritize by the commercial objective using the
[commercial finding format](review.md#commercial-findings), with task-blocking
defects surfaced first. Use [experimentation](experimentation.md) when selecting
metrics or evaluating results. With a screenshot only, identify visible issues
and propose checks for destinations and form behavior; do not invent a completed
journey inspection or measured revenue impact.
