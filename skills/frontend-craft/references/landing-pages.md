# Landing pages

Read for public marketing pages or reviews of a visitor's conversion journey.
Keep operational dashboard patterns separate from marketing composition.

## Establish the visitor's decision

Identify audience, traffic context when known, offer, primary next action, and
the factual evidence available. Let Impeccable own visual direction. Reuse brand
tokens and applicable public-site components without forcing the dashboard shell
or its density onto a landing page.

Build a content sequence that answers the visitor's actual questions: what is
offered, why it matters, how it works, whether to trust it, what it costs where
relevant, and what happens next. The sequence follows the decision; it is not a
mandatory nine-section template or a fixed word-count formula.

## Make each element earn its place

- Make the principal promise and next action easy to locate. Use supporting
  text only when it adds necessary information rather than paraphrasing a title.
- Choose imagery that demonstrates the product or desired outcome. A clear
  product screenshot can be stronger evidence than decorative lifestyle imagery.
- Use real, attributable proof supplied or verified for the task. Do not invent
  testimonials, customer logos, metrics, scarcity, guarantees or certifications.
- Give one action clear priority where that matches the decision. Secondary
  paths may be useful for different readiness levels; distinguish their purpose.
- Explain what clicking does. Request only information needed at that stage,
  and make failure recovery and the post-submit next step understandable.
- Answer material objections near the decision they affect. Avoid repeating
  generic trust badges or adding urgency without a factual reason.

Check reading order, hierarchy, contrast, meaningful images, mobile composition
and keyboard flow. Preserve the core offer when adapting layouts; avoid overlays
or sticky actions that obstruct content or focus. Load the interaction reference
for forms/controls and the motion reference if movement is involved.

## Review by evidence

When traffic/ad context exists, compare the promise before the click with the
page and the action after it. Review the whole requested path, including form
errors and confirmation, rather than ending at an attractive hero.

Separate observed usability defects from conversion hypotheses. Analytics can
locate drop-off but does not by itself establish the cause. State missing data
and distinguish a proposed experiment from a proven improvement. Do not predict
percentage lifts, infer real-user speed from a single local trace, or treat
insufficient traffic as a reason to skip a useful qualitative review.

Prioritize concrete changes by likely user impact, supporting evidence and
effort. Name the element, failure, proposed correction and what would validate
it. Respect the offer and approved copy; improving layout does not authorize
changing commercial terms or installing analytics infrastructure.
