# Product growth, offers and sales

Read for “help me sell more,” business strategy, a full commercial product audit,
offer design or an assisted-sales problem. Use the brief and source terms in
[commercial strategy](commercial-strategy.md). This guide connects the twelve
Revenue-Centric Design themes to business decisions, with additional sales
application by Frontend Craft; see [sources](../SOURCES.md#standalone-strategy-and-sales-in-060).
It works with a product description, interviews, sales records or business metrics;
a website or codebase is optional.

## Diagnose the constraint to profitable growth

Start from the requested result and horizon: first paying customers, more qualified
deals, higher contribution, improved retention or account expansion. Distinguish
more revenue from more profit and cash collected. Recover what is already known
about the audience, offer, alternatives, buying path and delivery economics.
Ask for the missing facts that would change the immediate decision; give useful
provisional actions when the remaining uncertainty can be stated.

Use comparable cohorts to locate the constraint. A rate alone does not establish
its cause; examine conversations, observed behavior and account outcomes.

| Observed pattern | Investigate | Candidate action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Few relevant prospects | Audience access, problem urgency, channel/message fit | Narrow a reachable segment and test a concrete acquisition approach |
| Attention or signups without purchase intent | Buyer fit, costly problem, offer and proof | Validate the buying trigger and test a specific paid offer |
| Qualified buyers stall before commitment | Objections, decision roles, risk, price, proposal and procurement | Change the sales process, evidence, package or evaluation path |
| Buyers pay but fail to obtain value | Setup, implementation capacity, expectations and time-to-value | Improve first-value delivery or qualify the offer more accurately |
| Customers get value and then leave | Recurring need, reliability, changing budget, champion loss and billing | Repair the relevant retention cause or reconsider the recurring model |
| Revenue grows while cash or margin deteriorates | Acquisition/payback, service cost, discounts and payment timing | Rework pricing, limits, delivery economics or channel mix |
| Successful accounts need more but do not expand | Capacity, buying authority, plan fit and awareness | Make a relevant expansion offer tied to demonstrated need |

Choose the intervention from the diagnosis. More features, ad spend, a price cut
and a redesign are competing options, each needing a mechanism and evidence.
Prefer a bounded validating action when uncertainty is the limiting factor.

## Cover the full commercial scope when requested

For a full strategic audit, assess each dimension below. Record existing evidence,
the decision or material unknown, and whether it currently limits the objective.
Load each specialty needed to support that decision. A focused sales question
uses the relevant subset; an exhaustive audit marks a dimension not applicable
with a reason instead of silently skipping it.

| Checkout and forms | Can a qualified buyer complete the commitment with understood effort, risk and terms? | [Checkout](checkout.md) |
| Onboarding and activation | How will the customer obtain the first useful result, with or without assisted implementation? | [Activation](activation.md) |

| Dashboards and data visualization | What evidence helps the relevant role make the next business decision? | [Dashboards](dashboards.md) |
| Behavioral science | Which default, framing or commitment mechanism addresses the actual barrier? | [Behavioral design](behavioral-design.md) |
| Product strategy and features | What should be built, improved, deferred or retired to strengthen the offer? | [Product strategy](product-strategy.md) |

Coverage retains useful source mechanisms with contextual and evidence limits.
When the requested audit includes GTM operations, assess measurement/data trust,
qualification and handoff ownership, workflow reliability and cost using
[GTM measurement](gtm-measurement.md) and [GTM automation](gtm-automation.md).
An audit can recommend a manual repair; it need not implement automation.
An upstream benchmark or case result is not a promise for this product. For a
named mechanism, explain what it changes, when it fits and how to evaluate it;
the mechanism's name alone is not a recommendation.

## Make the offer worth buying

Define the offer as a target buyer with a triggering problem, a useful result,
delivery method, credible proof, scope, price and next commitment. Distinguish
the buying promise from a feature list. Explain why this route is preferable to
the actual alternative: manual work, an incumbent, another tool or doing nothing.
Use buyer language and awareness from research or sales evidence.

Check the barrier before changing the package. Missing proof can call for a
demonstration or paid pilot; difficult implementation can call for a scoped setup
service; an infrequent job can call for a project purchase instead of subscription.
Evaluate the delivery and support burden of each option. A guarantee, bonus or
discount changes the offer's obligations and economics; draft it with explicit
conditions supported by the business rather than inventing available terms.

For a useful offer draft, state audience, problem/result, differentiation, what
is included, limits, supported proof, price or research scenario, and next step.
Separate delivered capabilities from proposed additions. Use the
[claim inventory](landing-pages.md#commercial-brief-and-claim-inventory) for sales
claims as well as website copy. Use [pricing economics](pricing-economics.md)
for price bounds and [monetization](monetization.md) for tiers and trial design.

## Improve the assisted buying process

Trace reached prospects → conversations → qualified opportunities → evaluation
or demo → proposal → decision → payment → first value. Adapt the stages to the
real buying process; record counts, elapsed time, loss/no-decision reasons and
next commitments. Separate contact response from qualification and a signed
agreement from collected payment. Include acquisition and sales labor when
comparing this motion with self-service; cost definitions must be consistent.

Identify user, champion, economic buyer and approver where different. Qualify the
problem's impact, urgency, existing solution, evaluation criteria, implementation
needs and buying process. A request for a feature is neither a purchase commitment
nor an automatic roadmap priority. Use [product strategy](product-strategy.md)
for repeated demands that affect fit or scope.

Design discovery around recent behavior: what happened, how the buyer handles it,
what it costs, why change now and what prevents a decision. Avoid questions that
only invite compliments. For a demo, show the buyer's actual job and the path to
a useful result, address the decisive uncertainty and agree on the next evaluation
or buying step. Feature tours are appropriate only where those details are needed
to evaluate the product.

Classify objections before responding: poor fit, unclear value, inadequate proof,
price/budget, timing, implementation risk or approval. Prepare an accurate answer
with evidence and a next action; discovery can reveal a genuine mismatch that
should disqualify the opportunity. A blanket discount can sacrifice margin without
resolving risk or urgency. Test a scoped pilot, package adjustment or implementation
plan when it addresses the actual objection and the economics support it.

Draft proposals around outcome, scope, responsibilities, supported proof, price,
payment terms, implementation and the decision step. Draft follow-up around the
buyer's unresolved question and agreed timing, with a useful piece of evidence.
Present outreach or follow-up text for use; sending it depends on the user's
existing authorization. A strategy request alone is not an instruction to contact
customers, spend money or change live commercial commitments.

## Deliver actions that can be used

For qualification, owner assignment, response expectations and stalled handoffs,
use [sales handoffs](gtm-automation.md#qualification-routing-and-handoffs). For
enrichment, CRM workflows, GTM agents or internal sales tools, use
[GTM automation](gtm-automation.md). For pipeline definitions, reconciliation,
attribution, scoring and operating reviews, use [GTM measurement](gtm-measurement.md).
These routes support both planning and explicitly requested implementation;
an offer draft or a narrow interface repair need not load them.

Return the diagnosed constraint and prioritized actions tied to the commercial
objective. Each action states the segment, evidence, proposed move, expected
mechanism, effort/cost or owner where known, success measure and next decision.
Use one coherent report; a sales or strategy review can use the
[commercial findings](review.md#commercial-findings) format without a UI location.

Include the concrete artifact the task needs: segment choice, offer draft,
positioning statement, interview or demo outline, objection responses, proposal,
channel experiment, pricing scenarios or a roadmap decision. A plan that only
says “improve positioning” or “talk to customers” is unfinished; specify the
message, audience, research question or offer to test.

For an acquisition test, define audience/access path, message/offer, next action,
effort or budget assumptions, observation window and continue/change/stop rule.
Use downstream purchase and retained contribution alongside channel diagnostics.
For low-volume products, use observed commitments, qualified conversations and
paid pilots rather than requiring an underpowered A/B test.

Separate completed analysis and drafted material from actions executed and
commercial results measured. Strategy is complete when the requested decision
and its usable next actions are supported with stated assumptions; implementation
and rendered checks apply only to interface changes actually requested.
