# Social channel: profile, intent, search, cadence and testing

Read when the user asks what to post, how to set up or fix a profile, how to
repurpose a long recording, or how to judge what is working. The aim is clients,
not audience size or creator income: a channel earns its place when it produces
profile visits, messages, calls and leads. Creator monetization (brand deals,
affiliate, shop) is out of scope. Video mechanics are in [social](social.md);
hooks in [hooks-and-scripts](hooks-and-scripts.md); claims and voice in
[copywriting](copywriting.md); audience and offer in [offer-brief](offer-brief.md).

The agent drafts. The user creates accounts, changes bios, posts and sends
messages.

## Profile and niche as a promise

- Niche, then sub-niche. A profile for one narrow audience filters the right
  people in and the wrong people out. One persona per profile; separate audiences
  deserve separate profiles. Choose the sub-niche from the offer brief, not from
  what is easy to film.
- Bio as a promise: one clear sentence on who you help, what you do and what
  they gain by following. The content converts; the bio confirms. Add a way to
  act: the link, or a keyword to message.
- Name and handle: put the searchable keyword in the name field, and keep the
  handle short and easy to say. (Instagram's search weighs several factors; treat
  specifics as unverified and re-check.)
- Photo: a clear face is easier to recognize than a logo in a fast feed, if the
  user is the brand.
- Three pinned videos for a visitor deciding whether to follow: who you are and
  what you do; proof or a short client story told truthfully; one that shows how
  you work or what a project looks like. Pinning keeps newcomers from landing on a
  random post.
- Link destination: one primary link to a landing page, ideally built with
  frontend-craft for this audience. The page should repeat the video's promise,
  show proof, and offer one action. Use a tagged URL per channel so visits can be
  attributed; measurement design is frontend-craft's. A generic list of links is a
  weaker first impression than one page that continues the conversation. Check the
  link works on a phone before it goes in a bio.
- Be consistent in name, face, tone and visual style so people recognize the
  account across platforms.

Check: a stranger can read the bio and three pinned videos and say who it is for,
what the user does and what to do next.

## Content intent per video

Give every video one intent and one stage of the buyer's path. This adapts the
growth, objection, connection and authority types and the discovery-to-advocacy
path used in the insta-master skill (MIT, SOURCES), without its unsourced
statistics.

| Buyer stage | Intent | Typical video |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Discovery (cold audience) | Growth | Short, broad-language video that speaks to the shared problem |
| Consideration | Connection and authority | Behind the work, process, a decision explained, a proof story |
| Conversion | Objection | A client result told truthfully, how pricing or process works, an honest answer to a common doubt |
| After the sale | Care | Onboarding tips, results review, a thank-you |
| Advocacy | Sharing | A video worth tagging or forwarding |

Per video, answer: which single intent, which stage, which format, and what the
viewer gets (not what you extract). Most of the feed should give something useful;
keep direct selling to a minority of posts. Do not use a percentage as law.

Authenticity beats polish. Record on a phone, in a place that fits the topic, with
good audio and simple cuts. Production quality should not outrun the idea.
Replicate the logic of formats that work in the niche (structure, pacing, hook
type), never someone's footage, script or voice. See
[rights-and-disclosure](rights-and-disclosure.md).

### Repurpose long recordings into atoms

One talk, call, demo or interview can feed weeks of posts.

1. Transcribe with word timestamps ([captions](captions.md)).
2. Mark five to ten self-contained moments: a quotable line, a short story, a
   tip, a before-and-after, a surprising fact, a behind-the-scenes beat. The user
   confirms consent for any other person's voice or image.
3. Cut each atom with a clean start; rewrite its hook with the three-part method
   in [hooks-and-scripts](hooks-and-scripts.md).
4. Write a standalone caption; the viewer has not seen the rest.
5. Burn in captions, export per [social](social.md), and spread posting over weeks.

## Search

People search inside platforms. The documented basics:

- Instagram uses keywords in content, caption, bio and hashtags for search
  (https://creators.instagram.com/blog/tips-for-improving-your-reach). YouTube
  Shorts search relies on metadata (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/11914225).
  Checked 2026-11-11.
- Put the topic words a buyer would type in the first line of the caption, in the
  on-screen text, in the spoken line and in the bio. Answer the question in the caption in short
  paragraphs instead of one line.
- Hashtags: use few and relevant ones. Hashtag following was removed on
  Instagram in December 2024 and a cap of five was reported in December 2025
  (https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagrams-removing-the-option-to-follow-hashtags/733155/,
  https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-implements-new-limits-on-hashtag-use/808309/;
  third-party reporting, re-verify). Put them in the caption, tied to the topic or
  place. Do not test whether a tag is banned by looking for a follow button; that
  button no longer exists.
- Shorts: write a plain, keyword-first title and description; add timestamps if
  the video is long.
- Alt text on image posts and covers helps accessibility.
- Discovery through AI assistants is a separate, fast-changing topic with no
  official guidance for these platforms. Do not promise it; a clear presence on the
  user's own site helps more (frontend-craft handles site-side discoverability).

## Cadence

No platform requires a posting rate. YouTube says Shorts have no required cadence
(checked 2026-11-11). Treat the following as hypotheses:

- Pick a rate you can keep with quality for at least a month. Three to seven
  posts a week is a common range in practitioner advice; steady beats bursts.
- More volume teaches faster only when each post is good enough to be shown.
- Do not delete posts that underperform; they are data and may be found later.
- Do not hunt a universal best time. Post when your audience is online and compare
  your own history.
- Keep one in five posts for testing a new format.
- Do not warm up, automate engagement, buy followers, or run coordinated
  accounts. Those risk the account.

## Cross-posting

- Make one master; tweak the hook per platform, not the whole video. Platform
  norms differ: a short question hook for Instagram, an assertive or entertaining
  first line for TikTok are practitioner guesses to test, not facts.
- Export a clean file from the project for each destination. Never repost a
  file carrying another platform's watermark; Instagram does not recommend
  content with visible watermarks.
- Keep captions, tags and links native to each platform; a link in a TikTok
  caption does not behave like one in a bio.
- If a hook does well on one platform, test it on another before assuming it
  transfers; audiences and distribution differ.
- Music: use the in-app library on the posted copy only. See
  [rights-and-disclosure](rights-and-disclosure.md).

## Respond to intent in messages

Message people who signaled interest: a comment with a keyword, a reply to a
story, a direct message. Reply fast, do not put a link in the first message,
give something useful, then offer a next step on an owned channel. No scraping,
no unsolicited cold messages, no automation that breaks platform terms. The user
sends all messages; the agent may draft replies.

## Test and learn

- Trial reels (Instagram): shown to non-followers first, with results after about
  24 hours and optional auto-share based on 72-hour views
  (https://creators.instagram.com/blog/instagram-trial-reels; checked 2026-11-11).
  Use them to compare hooks. On TikTok or YouTube use private or unlisted posts
  for checks, not for reach tests.
- Change one variable per test: the visual, or the first line, or the length, or
  the call to action. Re-write the bridge when you change the hook.
- A format wins by what it produces: profile visits, link clicks, direct
  messages, booked calls and leads, not by views. A high-view video that moves
  nothing is a top-of-funnel format, still useful but not a winner.
- Diagnose in order. Is the content eligible (Instagram's Account Status)? Do
  viewers stay past the first seconds? Do they keep watching, send or save? Do
  they visit the profile? Do they message or click? Fix the earliest weak step.
  The "true interest" survey model Meta described is for Facebook Reels, not a
  diagnostic for Instagram.
- Compare each video with the account's own median, not with benchmarks from
  other accounts.
- Review a post 30 days after publication to avoid reacting to early spikes.

### Outcome log

Keep one table per account, owned by the user. Append a row per post.

| Field | Note |
| --- | --- |
| Date, platform, link | |
| Format, hook type, intent, stage | From the script |
| Length | Seconds |
| 48-hour views, average watch time, percent viewed | From platform analytics |
| Sends, saves, comments, follows | |
| Profile visits, link clicks | |
| DMs, calls, leads | Count only those traced to the post, and how you know |
| Notes | What to change next |

Ask new leads "how did you hear about us" and record the answer; tagged links
miss people who search later. A winner is a format whose outcomes beat the median
on several posts, not one lucky video.

Check: the log shows at least the intent, the one variable changed and the
outcome, so the next video starts from evidence rather than taste.
