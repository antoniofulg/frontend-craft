# Hooks, structure and scripts for short video

Read when writing or reviewing the opening and shape of a short video before the
storyboard ([storyboard](storyboard.md)). It covers what platforms say about
retention, the hook as three parts, story structures and a script template. For
claims, voice and wording rules use [copywriting](copywriting.md); for the
audience, offer and verbatim buyer language use [offer-brief](offer-brief.md).
Do not write a hook before the brief names who it is for and what they want.

## What platforms say, and what is folklore

Official guidance is thin. Use it as direction, and log your own results
(see [social-channel](social-channel.md)).

Official or near-official (checked 2026-11-11):

- Instagram: each surface ranks separately, and Reels mostly reaches people who
  do not follow you. Signals include likes, comments, shares and saves, and how
  fast they arrive (https://creators.instagram.com/blog/tips-for-improving-your-reach).
  Eligible content goes first to a small non-follower audience and expands with
  engagement. Content with visible watermarks, reposts, clickbait or engagement
  bait is not recommended (https://creators.instagram.com/recommendations-and-originality).
  Mosseri's 2025 summary of top signals (watch time, likes, sends) reaches us
  through press coverage, not a primary page
  (https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/).
- TikTok performance-ad guidance, the closest official craft advice: state the
  main message in the first 3 s, land the hook within 6 s, show people, keep a
  native feel, and structure as hook, proposition, clear call to action. TikTok
  says its feed is sound-on and recommends using sound
  (https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/creative-best-practices).
- YouTube Shorts: ranking looks at the share of viewers who chose to watch versus
  swiped away, average view duration and average percentage viewed, then likes
  and surveys. YouTube has no required posting cadence
  (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/11914225).
- Meta's survey-based "true interest" model describes Facebook Reels, not
  Instagram (https://engineering.fb.com/2026/01/14/ml-applications/adapting-the-facebook-reels-recsys-ai-model-based-on-user-feedback/).

Folklore to treat as hypotheses, never as gates: a fixed "3-second rule";
specific retention thresholds in seconds; "watch a third of the duration"; "under
60 s drives most growth"; best posting times; likes-per-reach and
shares-per-reach targets; claims that trending music does or does not matter;
loop tricks that "work until the platform closes them"; "85% watch muted" and
"captions add 25 to 40% watch time". The sound-off figure traces to a 2016
Facebook-era report, and TikTok itself recommends sound. Design for both: speech
carries the content, captions make it understood muted.

For prospecting, the useful retention idea is this: the whole opening holds the
viewer, not one clever second. Decide the first sentence and the first frame
before you shoot.

Check: any number you cite is either from the platform's own page (named, dated)
or labelled a hypothesis.

## The hook is three parts

A video hook is what the viewer sees, hears and reads at the same moment:

| Part | Job |
| --- | --- |
| Visual (first 0 to 3 s) | What is on screen: an action, a result, the real interface, a face |
| Spoken line | The first sentence of the voice |
| Caption text | The on-screen header, so a muted viewer gets the claim |

The three must complement each other, not repeat. If the voice says "I stopped
paying for X" while the caption says the same words over a static talking head,
two parts are wasted. Split the work: the visual shows the cancellation, the
voice says the line, the caption names the alternative. Write all three columns
for every hook; a hook with one column filled is a third of a hook. This follows
the hook system in Corey Haines' ad-creative skill (MIT, see SOURCES).

### Generate from segment, motivation and format

Work top-down; hooks written without the upstream steps read like everyone
else's.

1. Segment: one slice of the audience with a shared situation, taken from the
   offer brief. Narrower is sharper.
2. Motivation: the single pain, desire or objection that moves that segment, in
   their own words. Use verbatim phrases from reviews, calls, comments or DMs the
   user supplied. If none exist, ask rather than invent.
3. Format: choose the delivery before the line: talking head, screen demo,
   voiceover over footage, before and after, street question, reaction. The same
   motivation reads differently in each.
4. Hook: now write the three parts for that cell.

Output a matrix so coverage is visible, and generate across cells instead of
rewording one:

```
| # | Segment | Motivation (source) | Format | Visual | Spoken line | Caption |
```

Ten hooks over ten cells teach more than thirty rewordings of one.

Opening moves worth rotating: a specific bold claim you can show; proof first
(the result on screen); a recognizable situation; the buyer's exact question; two
real options with a verdict; a belief the audience holds that you can honestly
challenge. Every promise must pay off in the body; a withheld noun that never
resolves is clickbait. Never use a fake stat, a fake testimonial or an
undisclosed before-and-after. Nothing here licenses a claim the brief cannot
support (see [copywriting](copywriting.md)).

### The bridge after the hook

Seconds 3 to 15 must extend the hook's premise. If the hook promises the cause,
the next beat explains the cause; it does not pivot to a brand story. When you
swap a new hook onto an old body, rewrite the bridge to match. Diagnose by part:
weak first-seconds viewing points to the visual and caption; viewers leaving
after the hook point to the bridge; few profile visits or messages point to the
promise or CTA.

## Pick a structure

| Structure | Shape | Fits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Problem to solution | Name the problem, show why it persists, show the fix, state the result | Services with a clear pain |
| Result-first tutorial | Show the finished result, then the steps to get it | Skills, workflows, how-tos |
| Story | Hook with the outcome, set the stakes, what happened, the result, one lesson | Case studies, founder stories |
| Screen demo | Hook with the outcome in the real product, one task, visible result, next step | Product walk-throughs (see [demo](demo.md)) |

Story arc as a rough 45 to 60 s guide: hook 0 to 3 s, setup to 15 s, journey to
about 45 s, resolution, then lesson and call to action. These timings are a
hypothesis, not a rule.

Rules for all structures:

- One idea and one call to action per video. A follow, a comment on a specific
  question, a link in bio or a message keyword; not all of them.
- The video must make sense without sound: captions plus a visual that matches
  the speech.
- Cut breaths and dead air; keep the pace the topic needs, not a fixed cut rate.
- Show the viewer's job, not your process, unless the process is the point.

## Record in flow, then move the best line first

Do not script every word. Record the idea in one natural pass of two or three
minutes, in a setting that matches the topic, then transcribe it
([captions](captions.md)). Find the best sentence: the one that states the
promise or the surprise. Move it to the start, cut what it makes redundant, and
rewrite the bridge. Read the final script aloud and replace any line you would
not say in conversation. Do not delay a direct answer just to build suspense.

## Script template per beat

Fill one row per beat. The storyboard that goes to the user for approval is built
from these rows ([storyboard](storyboard.md)).

```markdown
## Video: <working title>
Destination: Reels / TikTok / Shorts / web   Length: <s>   Format: <talking head / screen demo / ...>
Audience segment: <from the brief>   Motivation (verbatim source): <...>   Intent: <growth / objection / connection / authority>

| Beat | Time | Visual | Audio (spoken line) | Overlay / caption | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hook | 0 to 3 s | | | | complements, does not repeat |
| Bridge | 3 to 10 s | | | | extends the hook's premise |
| Body 1 | | | | | one idea |
| Body 2 | | | | | |
| Result | | | | | proof with its source |
| CTA | last 3 to 5 s | | | | one action, matching the destination |

Footage needed: <list>   Graphics: <list>   Music: owned or cleared only
Claims on screen and their source: <list>
```

Keep post captions free of generated-sounding tells: no "it's not X, it's Y"
reveals, no stacks of one-line paragraphs, no emoji bullets, no engagement bait
such as "agree?" or "thoughts?", no stock phrases. State the point directly with
a specific fact. Details are in [copywriting](copywriting.md).

Check: each hook has three distinct parts, the bridge follows from it, every
claim has a source, and the sound-off version makes sense.
